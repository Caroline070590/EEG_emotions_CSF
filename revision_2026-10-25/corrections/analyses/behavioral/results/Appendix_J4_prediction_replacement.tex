To compare assigned labels with reported experience across videos, we used 28 leave-one-video-out folds, retaining the same participants in training and testing. All four models included participant indicators: the reference model used these alone; the label model added assigned condition; the rating model added valence and arousal; and the combined model included both. Within each fold, each participant's outcome mean and population standard deviation were estimated from the 27 training videos only and applied to training and held-out observations. Rating predictors were likewise standardized using training observations only. A constant training outcome was assigned a scale of one original unit; this occurred for one participant's Lag measure. Predictions were transformed back to original units before the principal RMSE comparison; training-standardized errors were retained as a sensitivity analysis (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s9-loov}). Neither rating-only nor combined models differed from the assigned-label model after correction across the 12 paired comparisons (all $q\geq0.611$). The same conclusion held for training-standardized errors (all $q\geq0.179$). These exploratory comparisons relate final-second EEG descriptors to assigned categories and subsequent whole-video ratings; the training sets overlap across held-video folds.
