Scientific result (for the supplement; the original one-second main remains the focus)

SEED-VII sessions each contained five of the seven conditions, so holding out source programs does not by itself eliminate session-associated information. Under the same 54 source-held-out folds, a session-only reference assigning equal probability to conditions present in the training observations of the test session had 20.0% expected balanced accuracy. Synchronization and power achieved 29.5% observed balanced accuracy.

An additional sensitivity analysis preserved session-by-condition composition by permuting condition labels among 47 single-condition source programs in eight strata with identical four-session clip-count profiles. All excerpts from each program remained together; seven programs comprising 24 clips remained fixed because their profiles were unique or they contained two conditions. The feature normalization, classifier and 54 source-held-out folds were unchanged. Across 1,000 refits, the null mean was 13.2%, its 95th percentile was 16.6%, and none of the null scores reached the observed 29.5% (Monte Carlo p = 0.001). This result supports condition-associated information under the stated session-conditional exchangeability assumptions, without isolating emotion from other stimulus properties. The sensitivity applies to synchronization plus power; the original signed-edge and recurrence decoding nulls did not preserve session composition.

Optional methods/control detail, to retain in supplementary documentation

A training-session frequency-majority classifier obtained 0% balanced accuracy, including when maxima were tied uniformly. Withholding a source reduced the frequency of its condition in the corresponding training sessions, so this poor reference score cannot be interpreted as removal of session confounding. Sampling from the training-session frequencies yielded 14.3% expected balanced accuracy, and an inverse-training-class-frequency session rule yielded 17.1% when ties were averaged. The uniform training-support reference (20.0%) was therefore reported as an explicit stochastic benchmark alongside the restricted permutation sensitivity. These are analytically expected accuracies of stated probability rules, not deterministic EEG classification scores; no 23.81% participant-held-out score was subtracted from the source-held-out result.

Supplementary source-held-out prediction in SEED-VII remained above a permutation baseline that preserved session-specific condition counts, within the stated source-exchangeability constraints (Appendix C).

Reviewer-response proposal

We now account for the association between session and condition in the held-source analysis, as well as in participant-held-out comparisons. Under the original 54 source-held-out folds, synchronization and power reached 29.5% balanced accuracy. A new conditional null kept related excerpts together and preserved every session-by-condition count by exchanging labels among source programs with the same session-count profile. The observed score exceeded all 1,000 refitted null scores (mean 13.2%, p = 0.001). A training-only session-support reference had 20.0% expected balanced accuracy. Appendix C describes the 47 exchangeable sources and seven fixed sources, the alternative session-only reference rules, and the remaining stimulus-content limitation. This sensitivity concerns synchronization plus power and does not retroactively validate the other feature sets against a session-preserving null.
