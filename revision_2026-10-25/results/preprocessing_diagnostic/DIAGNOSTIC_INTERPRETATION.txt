FACED EEGNet preprocessing audit — interpretation for author review
Verified 25 October 2026

Main finding
The 10-s EEGNet baseline is sensitive to preprocessing. With the same architecture,
training rules, participant split and random seeds, full-trial Fourier resampling
followed by training-only normalization (C) produced higher window and trial
balanced accuracy than the original independent-window pipeline (A) in all three
seeds. These results support a concrete preprocessing sensitivity explanation for
part of the weak baseline; they do not recover the published EEGNet recipe or
explain the complete difference from its reported 40.90% score.

Prespecified recipes
A: process each 10-s window independently, polyphase resample 250 to 200 Hz,
   apply an additional 1–45 Hz filter, then use training-only channel z-scores.
B: Fourier resample each complete 30-s trial to 200 Hz, split into three 10-s
   windows, add no further filter, and divide amplitudes by 100.
C: use the identical prepared signal as B, with training-only channel z-scores.
All recipes use the same 30 channels and EEGNet-8,2 architecture, Adam settings,
weighted loss and validation-based checkpoint selection. No validation refit was
performed. Training/validation/test participants are fixed at 80/20/23.

What the contrasts establish
C versus B isolates amplitude normalization within the full-trial Fourier
pipeline. Its positive differences across all seeds support the contribution of
training-based normalization under that pipeline. A versus C holds normalization
fixed but changes the additional filter, resampling method and temporal processing
boundary together. Their separate contributions have not been identified. B
versus A gives mixed results, so the full-trial-resampling-plus-/100 recipe did
not consistently improve on A. C also has higher validation accuracy
than A and B in each seed; the observed pattern is not confined to test scores.

Scope and interpretation
The audit was designed after examining earlier results, and its participants and
videos have appeared in previous experiments. All nine diagnostic fits were then
specified together and retained, with checkpoint selection confined to validation
participants and one final test pass per fit. No recipe was selected or relabeled
as a reproduction of a published result. The exact published EEGNet training
recipe is unavailable in the inspected repositories, and original 32-channel
FACED files were not available for this audit.

Full-trial Fourier resampling is nonlocal within the 30-s trial. The nominal 10-s
model inputs therefore have different temporal preprocessing context across
recipes. All windows from a participant remain in the same split, but this audit
does not hold a strictly 10-s information boundary constant. It also changes the
participant protocol from the original five-fold matched experiment, so the
within-audit paired contrasts are the appropriate comparisons.

Intervals are descriptive, conditional on each fitted model, and do not include
training uncertainty or multiplicity adjustment. No permutation test or new-video
generalization claim is made. The figure's dotted 11.1% line is the nominal
nine-class reference, not an empirical permutation null.

Implication for the paper
Retain the original matched experiment and describe its feature increments as
established under the shared preprocessing protocol. The audit shows that its
10-s raw-EEG baseline does not characterize the full achievable performance of
EEGNet. Augmented models were not retrained under recipe C, so this audit does
not determine whether their increments persist under that preprocessing. It also
does not replace the paper's separate unseen-video GNN evaluation.

If the manuscript includes the matched EEGNet comparisons, disclose this material
sensitivity briefly in that section and report all nine fits in the supplement.
Suggested sentence: "EEGNet performance was sensitive to temporal preprocessing
in a separate FACED analysis; the reported feature increments are established
under the common preprocessing protocol used for the matched comparisons."
The supplement can quantify C–A as 6.8–9.2 percentage points and state the bundled
processing changes and exploratory scope. An author-only note is sufficient only
if these new matched EEGNet comparisons are not included or used to support a
claim in the submitted paper.
